# RAGWire Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

RAGWire is committed to a welcoming, respectful, and inclusive community.
Everyone who uses, contributes to, or maintains the project should be able to
participate without harassment or discrimination.

This commitment applies regardless of age, disability, ethnicity, gender
identity or expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity or
orientation.

## Expected behavior

All community members, including maintainers, are expected to:

- Communicate with empathy, patience, and respect.
- Welcome newcomers and support people with different backgrounds and levels
  of technical experience.
- Give constructive, specific feedback about ideas, code, documentation, and
  behavior.
- Discuss disagreements in good faith, explain technical decisions, and remain
  open to other perspectives.
- Respect personal boundaries, accessibility needs, and requests to end contact.
- Accept feedback, acknowledge mistakes, and take responsibility for their impact.
- Respect the time and effort of contributors, reviewers, and maintainers.
- Protect privacy and confidentiality when sharing examples, bug reports, logs,
  or evaluation results.

Because RAGWire processes documents and retrieves their contents, contributors
must take care when sharing source files, document chunks, metadata, prompts,
model responses, and vector-store exports. Use synthetic or appropriately
redacted examples, and share only material you are authorized to disclose.
Remove credentials, API keys, access tokens, and sensitive personal or
proprietary information before posting.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, threats, or encouragement of violence.
- Sexualized language or imagery, unwanted sexual attention, or sexual advances.
- Personal attacks, insults, derogatory comments, or deliberate humiliation.
- Trolling, bad-faith disruption, or repeated attempts to derail collaboration.
- Continuing unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information
  without permission.
- Deliberately sharing confidential documents or sensitive data without
  authorization.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern, supports a reporter, or
  participates in a conduct review.
- Other conduct that reasonably creates a hostile or unsafe environment.

Respectful criticism, disagreement, and evidence-based discussion are welcome.
Participants should challenge ideas without attacking the people behind them.

## Scope

This code applies to project spaces, including issues, pull requests, code
reviews, discussions, documentation, and project communication channels and
events where available.

It also applies when someone officially represents RAGWire in public, such as
through a project account, an appointed community role, or a designated speaking
engagement. It does not grant maintainers authority over unrelated personal
activity.

## Reporting concerns

Report conduct concerns privately to
**[uditmerit@gmail.com](mailto:uditmerit@gmail.com)**.

You may report an incident you experienced or witnessed. Include as much of the
following as you are comfortable providing:

- What happened, when it occurred, and where it took place.
- The people or accounts involved.
- Relevant links, screenshots, or other supporting information.
- Any immediate concerns or support you are requesting.
- A safe way to contact you for clarification or follow-up, if desired.

You do not need to provide a complete account before raising a concern. Do not
post sensitive incident details in public issues or pull requests, and redact
unrelated personal information or confidential documents from supporting
evidence.

If a concern involves the reporting contact, use another uninvolved maintainer's
published private contact method where one is available. Abusive accounts or
content on GitHub may also be reported through
[GitHub's reporting process](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
GitHub handles those reports separately from project moderation.

## Handling reports

Maintainers will review reports as promptly as practical and handle them
discreetly. Information should be shared only with people who need it to assess
the concern or implement a response. Absolute confidentiality cannot be
guaranteed.

The review should consider the available evidence, context, severity, impact,
and any relevant pattern of behavior. Where appropriate and safe, the people
involved should have a reasonable opportunity to provide relevant information.
A maintainer who is the subject of a report must not decide its outcome; an
uninvolved maintainer should handle the review where one is available.

Good-faith reporting must not result in retaliation, even when the available
evidence does not establish a violation.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may moderate content and participation within project spaces.
Responses should be proportionate to the conduct and may include:

1. **Correction:** Clarifying expectations, requesting that behavior stop, and
   removing or editing inappropriate content.
2. **Warning:** A written notice describing the violation, required changes,
   and consequences of further misconduct.
3. **Temporary restriction:** A time-limited restriction on participation or
   contact in project spaces, with conditions for returning where appropriate.
4. **Permanent restriction:** Removal from project participation for severe
   misconduct or a sustained pattern of violations.

These responses are guidelines rather than a mandatory sequence. Serious
incidents may warrant immediate restrictions without earlier warnings.

Where appropriate, maintainers should communicate the decision, its reason,
duration, and any conditions for returning, while respecting the privacy of
those involved.

## Reconsideration and accountability

A person affected by an enforcement decision may request reconsideration by
emailing the reporting address with relevant context or new evidence. Where
available, an uninvolved maintainer should review the request.

Maintainers are subject to the same standards as other participants and are
responsible for applying this code fairly and consistently.
